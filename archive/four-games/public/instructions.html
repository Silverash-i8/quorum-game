Cell Out

Player instructions. A business game for 3 or 4 players, about 45 minutes. Each player runs a company that builds battery packs for e-bikes and scooters, from 2027 to 2031.

1The aim

Finish 2031 with the highest Enterprise Score. Money counts, and so do your Reputation, your Sustainability and the technology you own.

Enterprise Score = net worth + Reputation + Sustainability + 2 per patent + 1 per trade secret

Net worth is your cash, minus your debt, plus £3m for each production line. Cutting corners makes money early. It rarely wins.

2What you need

  • One laptop running the game, ideally on a projector or TV. This is the big screen that everyone watches.
  • One phone each, on the same Wi-Fi or hotspot as the laptop. No app to install: the game runs in the phone's browser.
  • One datasheet and a pen each.

3Starting a game

  1. The host opens localhost:3000/host on the laptop. The big screen shows a QR code and a four-letter game code.
  2. Each player scans the QR code with their phone camera (or types the address shown), enters a company name and presses Join.
  3. With fewer than 3 people, the host can press Add a practice bot to fill a seat.
  4. The host chooses the time allowed for each decision (90 seconds is a good start) and presses Start the game.

Every company starts the same: £12m cash, no debt, 1 production line, Reputation 5, Sustainability 3, Cyber 0. Write these in the Start column of your datasheet.

Three tips for your first game. Keep enough cash after the boardroom to buy cells: a company that makes nothing earns nothing. Do not make more units than customers will buy: the extra sells at a loss. Buy protection (QA, Cyber) before the risk card arrives, not after.

Press investigation. After the risk cards, the game rolls a die for every hidden Skeleton. On a 1 or 2 it is exposed: you pay £5m and lose 2 Reputation. A shortcut gains you £2m; being caught costs far more.

10Year-end accounts

The game does the sums. Your phone shows them in this order:

  1. Sales, plus clearance of unsold units and any shortcut income.
  2. Costs: cells, £1m per line to run, investments (every action except a new line), loan interest, fines and incidents.
  3. Profit before tax, then tax of £1m for every full £4m of profit. A new production line is a capital purchase: it comes out of cash but not out of profit.
  4. Cash at end of year. If it is below zero, the bank forces £5m emergency loans on you until it is not, and you lose 1 Reputation.

11The end of the game

  1. Final audit. After 2031, the game rolls a die for every Skeleton still hidden. On a 1, 2 or 3 it is exposed: £5m and 2 Reputation.
  2. Climate crisis. If the Climate track has reached 30, every company loses 5 points, and the company that bought the most Valley cells loses 5 more.
  3. Score. The big screen shows every company's Enterprise Score. A company on Reputation 0 has its score halved. If scores are tied, the higher Reputation wins.

12If something goes wrong

  • A phone loses the game. Reopen the same address. It returns to your company. If that fails, join again with the game code and exactly the same company name.
  • Someone is slow or has left. The timer closes the round, or the host presses Close this round now. A company that did not answer does the dilemma properly, takes no actions, and orders Nordic cells it can afford.
  • The laptop restarts. Start the game again and reopen the host page in the same browser. Games in progress are saved.

13Why the game works this way

Every decision touches more than one part of the business:

  • Supply chain and sustainability. Cheap unaudited cells raise your margin, and also your carbon levy, your recall risk and the Climate track that every company shares.
  • Intellectual property and data. A patent costs more and is public. A trade secret is cheaper and hidden, but a data breach or a rival's filing can take it. Good cyber security protects data and lets you charge more.
  • Finance. Loans fund growth and cost interest. Lines are assets. Running out of cash costs Reputation.
  • Ethics and risk. Shortcuts pay now and become hidden liabilities. Each risk card has a control you could have bought earlier.

Reputation links them all. You lose it through ethics, risk and data failures. You use it to be served first by suppliers, and it counts in the final score.
